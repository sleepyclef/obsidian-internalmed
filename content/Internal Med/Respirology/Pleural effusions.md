

Parapneumonic effusions
![[Screenshot 2026-09-26 at 5.41.51 PM.png|654]]
- Classification
	- uncomplicated = no loculations/ septations, result of pleural inflammation from the near by infection, exudative (d/t inflammation) but Ø infected
	- complicated = bacteria has gotten into the fluid, but still cx can be negative, loculations on imaging (possible), need chest drain 
	- empyema = frank pus, +++WBC count, cx positive, need chest drain 
	- ![[Pasted image 20260926193806.png|200]]
- technically, if suspect parapneumonic effusion, should sample effusion to make sure it is not complicated/ empyema if it is big enough to tap (>1cm on CXR/US)
	- CMAJ 2018 "If a parapneumonic effusion is suspected, a diagnostic aspiration must be promptly performed to identify patients with a complicated effusion that requires drainage."
	- Effusions too small to be sampled can be followed radiologically and sampled if they enlarge. → if CXR/imaging says "small" can just leave it and monitor, tx same as regular PNA 
	- rigorous meta-analysis that showed that pleural pH is the best marker of complicated parapneumonic effusions (pleural glucose being the second-best marker).
	- if strongly suspect complex effusion based on imaging/US/labs then send for drain and get samples when drain put in
- pig tail is as good as large bore surgical chest tube (COREIM) - IR vs thoracics
