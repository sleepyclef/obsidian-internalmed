<u>Phlegmon vs Abscess </u>
Phlegmon: Diffuse inflammation/infection without organized pus collection → harder to mx since Ø drainable, Abx
	can evolve into abscess
	if Ø clinical improvement, consider repeat imaging
Abscess: Localized, walled-off pus collection (rim-enhancing on CT) → consider drainage + Abx. 
	tissue has undergone liquefactive necrosis and pus accumulates

<u>Sinus tracts</u>
sinus tract = 

anything that connects 2 tissues that are not connected otherwise 
	fistula = from something that has a lumen and going into any space (other lumen, outside etc)
	otherwise it is a sinus tract 
sinus tracts are generally from area of abnormal tissue (inflammation, infection, malig) into an external space (skin) or lumen space 
	3 processes that can lead to a sinus tract: (1) infection (usually chronic eg. bone and joint/ prosthesis infections) (2) non-infxn inflammation (3) malignancy 
pres'n
	usually will have constant drainage from the exit of the tract from the skin
if have a sinus tract from a prosthetic joint; it is actually indication to do a whole revision since indicates chronic infection 
similarly, if have sinus tract near CIED site, indication that there is an underlying infection
