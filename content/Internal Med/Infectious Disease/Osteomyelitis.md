Osteomyelitis: Pathogenesis & Empiric Abx
	Empiric abx guided by mechanism/source of infection, not comorbidities alone.
	Hematogenous OM: Usually monomicrobial (_S. aureus_ most common). Empiric: Vanco ± CTX (if GNB coverage needed).
	Contiguous OM: Depends on source/location:
	    - Foot: Chronic ulcers/DFI → often polymicrobial (GPC, GNB ± anaerobes), esp ischemia/necrosis. Empiric: Vanco + pip-tazo if severe; narrower if mild.
	    - Leg/thigh: Abscess extending to bone → usually _S. aureus_, unless ulcers, trauma, surgery. Empiric: Vanco often sufficient.
	    - Pelvis/sacrum: Pressure ulcers, GI/GU source → often polymicrobial incl anaerobes. Empiric: Vanco + pip-tazo.
	DM ≠ polymicrobial OM. Diabetic thigh OM ≠ diabetic foot OM.
	Severe infection → broader empiric coverage initially, then narrow per cultures. Source control essential.
	-
	Ex Case: 66M, T2DM, controlled HIV, prior MRSA abscess, now large spontaneous thigh abscess (300 cc drained) + suspected greater trochanter OM. Likely _S. aureus_ with contiguous spread to bone (vs primary OM → abscess). No clear risk factors for polymicrobial infection despite DM → vanco alone reasonable pending cultures if clinically stable.
