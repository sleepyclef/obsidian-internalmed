- pt endorsing sx of freq/ urgency/ dysuria/ incont/ nocturia/ suprapubic tend
- male - consider prosatitis, esp if pelvic/ perineal pain, can evaluate w DRE 
- hx of resistant organisms? 

- uncomplicated = simple cystitis
- complicated (upper tract) = fever >38C (30% UTI+fever are bacteremic), HD unstable, bacteremia, flank pain/ CVA tend, CAUTI, pregnant, renal trx 
  
- UA, Ucx, Bcx
- Abdo US or CT AP w contrast if complicated (look for pyelo, stones, abscess etc)

<u>Classification</u>
New IDSA 2025 (https://www.idsociety.org/practice-guideline/complicated-urinary-tract-infections/)
	uncomplicated = infxn confined to bladder (cystitis) in afebrile women OR men 
		rare to have febrile response to simple cystitis (possible if other cause of fever) 
	complicated = infxn beyond bladder in women or men; pyelo, bacteremic, catheter associated (CAUTI), prostatitis

<mark style="background:rgba(240, 200, 0, 0.2)">Uncomplicated</mark>
Can be clinical dx w/out even UA - woman w dysuria + ↑freq w/out vag discharge/ irritation = >90% likelihood of UTI
UA
	pyuria (>10 WBC); absence of pyruria strongly argues against cystitis 
	nitrite and leuks +ve = sn 68-88%
	nitrites +ve esp w enterobacterales (ie. gram negatives that cause UTIs, convert nitrate to nitrite)
	bacteria - threshold CFU >100k or >1k if CAUTI
Micro = KEEPS (klebs, e coli, enterococcus - rarely causes true infxn, proteus, s saprophyticus)
	can assume it is one of these and tx empirically
	outpatient - get UCx if male, atypical sx, sx >48-72h despite abx, recur w/in 3mo 
	inpt will get Ucx anyway 
Tx empiric
	Nitro 100mg BID x5d (not if CrCl <40, avoid 1st trimester, poor upper tract) 
	Septra DS (160/800) BID x3d (avoid if local resistance >20%, avoid 1st trimester)
	Fosfomycin pack 3g x1 (poor upper tract)
	Cipro 250 BID x3d (500 BID if ESBL/ AmpC)
	alt = Amox-clav 875 BID x5-7d 
	inpatient = CTX 1g q24h 
	(look at prev Ucx/ hx resistant organisms)

<mark style="background:rgba(240, 200, 0, 0.2)">Complicated</mark>
Pyelo is a complicated UTI
	WBC cases on UA suggestive of pyelo
CAUTI; presence of foley in pt w UTI (ie. not just asx bacteruria) is complicated
	colonization in context of foley with no sx is asx CAUTI and should not be treated as with the usual rules 
	foley should be removed or replaced when feasible, before collecting urine culture (ie. need clean sample from new foley) and starting antibiotic therapy
Micro = KEEPS + serratia morg, providencia, PsA, citrobacter; GPs rare, if S aureus think bacteremia
	Need to get UCx if complicated signs
	Bcx to look for bactermia
	Enterococcus mainly inclues E faecalis and faecium
		Enterococcus is frequently isolated from urine without causing a true UTI, particularly in hospitalized patients, indwelling foley 
		can cause true UTI esp if complicated sigs 
		E. faecalis is usually ampicillin-susceptible; E. faecium is often resistant.
	ESBL = erta/mero 
	AmpC inducers (H'ECK' Yes) = Ø CTX or ceftaz, tazo also unreliable; erta/mero until susceptibility back 
		all of them can be UTI pathogens so relevant here
		(Hafnia alvei), Enterobacter cloacae, Citrobacter freundii, Klebsiella aerogenes, (Yersinia enterocolitica)
			Hafnia alvei (H) and Yersinia enterocolitica (Yes) are less commonly encountered, but still considered clinically relevant.
		Proteus (indole +ve; not mirabilis) used to be in MISPACE for AmpC inducer but less reliably inducible so got dropped
		Serratia and PsA also got dropped cause not reliably inducible
	Candiduria
		asymptomatic colonization common; often seen w chronic foley
		only treat if symptoms present OR neutropenic OR before urologic procedure
		Tx: Fluc 200-400mg (if susceptible) PO qd x14d _OR_ for resistant _C.glabrata_ or _C. krusei_, Amphotericin x1-7d
Imaging esp if ill, suspect obstruction, persistent sx
	Abdo US 
	CT AP w contrast (plain CT still v good for stones but contrast can help see other stuff)
Tx empiric
	PO/ outpatient 
		cipro 500 BID or levo 750 x5-7d 
		Septra DS BID x7-10d
		(can give IV CTX 1g x1 prior to PO tx)
	IV/ Inpt
		CTX 1g q24h 
			+/- ampicillin 2 g IV q6h _(enterococcal coverage should be considered for pyelonephritis in men, presence of catheter or in hemodyamic instability)_, or if known hx enterococcus
		pip-tazo 4.5g q8h or 3.375g q6h (renally dose if needed)
			covers enterococcus too so no need for amp
			covers PsA
			does Ø cover ESBL
		mero 1g q8h (septic/ shock) or erta 1g q24h (known ESBL esp if ucx past 6mo)
			erta wont cover APE (acinetobacter, PsA, enterococcus - but again acenobact, PsA not typical, and enterococcus Ø true UTI)
			mero covers acinetobacter, PsA, but also Ø reliable against enterococcus (esp faecium)
	Generally 7d total effective therapy if clinically improving (FQ 5-7d); includes IV + PO days. Longer if prostatitis, abscess, inadequate source control, or slow response.

<u>Other UTIs</u>
urethritis
	suspect with dysuria + urethral discharge/meatal irritation or STI exposure; can mimic bacterial UTI, but coinfection is uncommon.
	isolated urethritis vs concurrent UTI; sx overlap and both can coexist
		isolated urethritis → STI risk, discharge/meatal irritation ± dysuria, often negative routine urine C&S
		concurrent UTI (cystitis, pyelo) → additional frequency/urgency, suprapubic pain or fever/flank pain, with supportive urine C&S.
	Ix: first-catch urine GC/CT NAAT ± gonorrhea culture if discharge; routine urine C&S if concurrent bacterial UTI suspected.
	Tx: if empiric STI treatment indicated → CTX 500 mg IM x1 (gonorrhea) + doxycycline 100 mg PO BID x7d (chlamydia, if not excluded). If already on IV CTX for UTI, only add doxycycline; other UTI antibiotics do not reliably cover GC/CT.
prostatitis
	suspect in male with dysuria/frequency + fever, pelvic/perineal pain, painful ejaculation or urinary retention; prostate may be tender/boggy on DRE.
    Prostatitis vs other UTI
	    Cystitis → LUTS without systemic symptoms; pyelo → fever/flank pain/CVAT; 
	    prostatitis → fever + pelvic/perineal pain or prostate tenderness. 
	    Symptoms overlap; prostatic involvement may occur without classic findings.
    Ix: UA + urine C&S ± blood Cx if febrile/systemically ill; gentle DRE if indicated (NO prostatic massage); consider imaging for abscess if not improving.
    Tx: Stable → cipro 500 mg PO BID or Septra DS PO BID (if susceptible); severe/admitted → IV CTX or broader per UTI algorithm, then PO step-down. Treat ~2–4 weeks total (reassess at 14d).
    Impact on UTI Tx: Choose agents with adequate prostatic penetration (FQ/TMP-SMX preferred for PO); avoid nitrofurantoin/fosfomycin. Requires longer treatment than typical cUTI (~7d).

<u>Ileal conduit case</u>
ID staff note
	"Ms X has an ileal conduit, and that Hobbs catheter inserted in August drains into the same urostomy bag. She has no other means for urine to exit, meaning that all urine samples would need to be collected from the urostomy bag, where they will be mixed ileal conduit and Hobbs, and in contact with skin flora, making it likely that they will always be positive even in the absence of true urinary tract infection. I note that urine culture Aug 16 grew 4 organisms, including Lactobacillus (not a urinary pathogen, but likely from the ileal component of the conduit). On my examination, Ms. X is alert, NAD, not flushed or diaphoretic, and has no suprapubic or CVA tenderness. The stoma is pink and well perfused, without skin breakdown or surrounding cellulitis. There is no pus. The urostomy bag contains clear yellow urine without blood or clots. Therefore, in light of low suspicion of urinary tract infection and possible C. difficile, will not continue antibiotics at this time."
