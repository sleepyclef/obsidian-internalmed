<u>Epi</u>
	1/4 world infected, 4% HIV coinfection, 1% MDR 
	RF = homeless, incarceration, PWID, healthcare, Africa/ Asia/ Eastern Europe/ Latin America/ Russia, HIV+, prior TB/exposure

<u>Course</u>
when exposed and infected
	5% develop <u>primary TB</u> ( fever, chest pain, cough, arthralgia. CXR can be normal, have focal infiltrate, or ⊕ hilar LAD)
	95% just keep it quiet and have latent TB (aka TB infection)
		5% of those have reactivation later and get active TB aka TB disease (either pulmonary or extrapulmonary)
			reactivation risk 5% first 2y, 5-10% lifetime
			higher if 1+ risk factor: HIV, immunosupp, CKD (esp. RRT), DM, transplant, TNFα inhibitor, silicosis, malnutrition, tobacco, EtOH
			pulmonary TB =  fever, cough, hemoptysis, night sweats, wt loss; CXR often w/ posterior/apical involvement or cavitation (seen in 1/3 of pts, a/w ↑org. burden → ↑infectious, AFB⊕); more common than primary TB
			extrapulmonary TB = meningitis with basilar predominance, Pott disease (spinal), GU involvement, intestinal TB, pericarditis, scrofula
		95% never reactivate/ never get active TB disease

<u>Screening for Latent TB</u>
who 
	shouldn't just test in everyone 
	test for it when you are worried about TB reactivating and therefore would want to treat first if pt has latent TB (eg. before biologics)
Both IGRA and TST are 80-90% Sn and >95% Sp in immunocompetent, ↓Sn in immunocompromised.
	IGRA = preferred, more expensive, takes 2wks to come back, not affected by BCG 
	TST = cheaper, quicker (48-72h), may be affected by BCG after 1yo
		5+mm if HIV, prior TB hx, CXR c/w prior TB, silicosis, immunosuppression
		10+mm if DM, CKD, PWID 
		15+mm if Ø RF
		*Size reflects skin induration, <u>NOT</u> erythema.
Neither rules in/out active TB, can be discordant ~30% of the time. 
If ⊕ test w/ no risk factors, repeat either IGRA or TST prior to tx
If ⊕ test in ↑risk pt, proceed to tx.

	before tx active TB = c/s ID, send TB for drug sensitivity testing
Latent TB (TB infection) tx
	INH+RPT qw x12 (3HP) **OR** 
	rifampin x4mo (4R) **OR** 
	INH+RIF x3mo (3HR) **OR** 
	INH+B6 x6-9mo (6H, 9H, less preferred)
Active drug-susceptible pulm TB
	RIPE x6mo (standard) 
	newer
		4mo isoniazid(INH)-rifapentine(RPT)-moxifloxacin-pyrazinamide followed by 2mo INH-RPT-moxi equally efficacious as standard 6mo RIPE. 
	Sputum AFB smear/cx monthly until 2 consecutive cx/smear negative.
MDR TB
	suspect if previously treated, treatment failure, from prevalent area (India, China, Russia, S. Africa), or known exposure. 
	Treatment regimen depends on drug susceptibility profile; usually 12-24mo course. High mortality
	Quinolones 1st line w/ MDR-TB
	avoid in bacterial PNA if suspicious for active TB (↓dx yield & ↑risk of resistance)
HIV co-infection
	discuss timing of ART initiation w/ ID due to risk for IRIS
Extrapulmonary TB 
	highly variable presentation/therapy, duration depends on site of infection & response. 
	For CNS TB: 12mo tx, glucocorticoids confer 25% short term reduction in mortality
Medication side effects
	hepatotoxicity (INH, RIF, PZA)
	optic neuritis (EMB)
	peripheral neuropathy (INH → add pyridoxine (B6) with initiation of treatment)
	orange bodily fluids (RIF)
	numerous drug-drug interactions (especially RIF)
RIF/RPT remain widely used despite 2020 FDA announcement of nitrosamine impurities as perceived risk TB > cancer risk
<u>Interactions with cardiac meds</u>
	rifampin is the one to worry about (CYP450 and P-glycoprotein inducer)
		DOAC = ↓ drug exposure = use LMWH (or warfarin w monitoring - see below)
		warfarin = ↓ INR = may require substantial dose increases; frequent INR monitoring
		P2Y12i = avoid ticag (↓ efficacy), avoid clop (↑ active metabolite), prefer prasugrel 
		β-b, CCB = ↓ lvls = wont have as good HR / BP control 
		dig = ↓ lvls = monitor dig lvls & adjust (note: can be more potent when rifamp stopped)
		statins, variable effect, no preference 
		ACEi/ARBs (especially enalapril, losartan) = possible ↓ active metabolite exposure, monitor BP 
		amio = ↓ lvls 
	isoniazid (H): CYP inhibitor (opposite of rifampin), but rifampin's induction generally dominates; also adds hepatotoxicity concerns with statins

# ? TB
- Suspect with:
    - Pulmonary: subacute cough, hemoptysis, fever, night sweats, weight loss; upper-lobe infiltrates/cavitation
    - Extrapulmonary: lymphadenopathy, pleural effusion, vertebral osteomyelitis, meningitis, pericarditis, etc.
    - RF: birth/residence in high-incidence country, TB exposure, prior untreated TB infection, HIV/immunosuppression

- If suspected pulmonary/laryngeal TB → airborne isolation (negative-pressure room), notify IPAC
- CXR ± CT chest depending on presentation
- Sputum AFB smear + mycobacterial Cx ×3 (≥1 h apart); TB NAAT on ≥1 respiratory specimen
    - If unable to expectorate → induced sputum ± bronchoscopy
    - If extrapulmonary → sample involved site for AFB/Cx/NAAT ± histopathology; assess for concurrent pulmonary TB
- HIV test; baseline CBC, LFTs, Cr; additional investigations guided by presentation

- Interpretation:
    - AFB smear (+) → mycobacteria present, but not necessarily M. tuberculosis (could be NTM)
    - TB NAAT (+) → rapid evidence of M. tuberculosis; may also detect rifampin resistance
    - Mycobacterial Cx → gold standard, permits susceptibility testing
    - Negative smear/NAAT does NOT exclude active TB
    - IGRA/TST → evidence of TB infection, NOT diagnostic of active TB; cannot distinguish latent vs active disease

- Management:
    - If high suspicion → discuss empiric therapy with ID/TB service while awaiting Cx
    - Drug-susceptible disease: usually 2 months HRZE → 4 months HR (adjust to susceptibility/site)
    - Monitor drug toxicities, adherence and treatment response
    - Notify public health for suspected/confirmed active TB; de-isolation with IPAC

- Pearls:
    - Active TB can be pulmonary or extrapulmonary; latent TB has no evidence of active disease
    - Extrapulmonary TB is not necessarily contagious; assess pulmonary involvement
    - Rifampin: major drug interactions (DOACs, antiarrhythmics, etc.)
    - INH: hepatotoxicity, neuropathy (pyridoxine)
    - PZA: hepatotoxicity, hyperuricemia
    - EMB: optic neuropathy
    - Treatment duration differs by site (e.g., TB meningitis) and drug susceptibility

# CAP, hx TB
- DDx: bacterial CAP vs TB reactivation/recurrence vs infection related to post-TB structural lung disease (e.g., bronchiectasis).
- Clarify prior TB: active vs latent, pulmonary involvement, treatment completion, prior resistance; compare current/ prior CXR.
- Assess for active TB: subacute/chronic cough, hemoptysis, night sweats, weight loss, upper-lobe infiltrates/ cavitation, immunosuppression.
- If concern for active pulmonary TB:
    - Airborne isolation + notify IPAC
    - Sputum AFB smear/Cx ×3 + TB NAAT
    - ID consult
- Treat CAP concurrently: CTX 1–2 g IV daily + azithro 500 mg daily (if standard inpatient CAP); avoid FQ if possible when TB suspected, as may partially treat/mask TB.
- Consider PsA coverage if significant post-TB bronchiectasis/structural lung disease with relevant risk factors or prior PsA isolation.
- If adequately treated TB, acute typical CAP, no features concerning for active TB → manage as standard CAP; no TB-specific isolation/workup routinely needed.
