<u>Pearls about these micro-organisms</u>
Gram negatives
	Easy to kill/ tx
		Enterobacterales (group) = KEEPS (the urinary pathogens) (different than enterococcus) 
			E coli, Klebs, Proteus, Enterobacter, Serratia, Citrobacter 
			Primarily GI tract; normal gut flora, can translocate to urinary tract 
		Syndromes = UTI, intra-abdominal, possibly polymicrobial SSTI
		Tx: CTX if Ø resistant
	Hard to kill/ tx
		The resistant organisms
			Enivironmental organisms (PsA, acinetobacter, stenotrophomonas) evolved in soil alongside abx producing microorganisms (mainly fungi which is where we got alot of our abx from) and so can often be resistant/ MDR
			ESBL/AmpC Enterobacterales - gut flora as above but resistance acquired through abx exposure in healthcare
		Syndromes = UTI, intra-abdominal, HAP/VAP
		Tx = depends on what they are sensitive to
	Very hard = call ID 

Synrome determines the most likely organisms and thus the empiric treatment 
2 other v important thing that will help see the likely organism
1. Need to check if pt has a hx of a hard to treat organism!
2. Risk factors v important as well as can change the organism (hospitalized, immunocomp, IVDU)

β-hemolytic strep (ie. GAS, GBS, Group C/G) are universally susceptible to penicillin, that is why lab doesn't even report susceptibility, someitme needed if pt has penicillin allergy to know what else we can use

Nocardia will stain weakly for AFB 

Intracellular organisms (Mycobateria, resp atypicals etc.) cannot be gram stained because they don't have a cell wall; they need to go inside a cell to survive, that is also why β-lactams dont cover them

E coli and Proteus have less resistance 
Klebsiella is universally resistant to ampicillin

Ampicillin is the IV equivalent of Amoxicillin
Amp-sulbactam is IV equivalent of Amox-clav; we now have IV amox-clav though

<u>Abx</u>
Penicillins (gram-positive predominant)
- Penicillin is mostly for strep, staph aureus produces penicillinase, also good for syphillis (T pallidium) 
- Cloxacillin was lab created specifically designed to fill the gap of stap aureus; now used for MSSA if can't use cefazolin (but cefazolin preferred as per SNAP trial) 
- Amoxicillin is an amino-penicillin, Ampicillin IV as well,  start having a bit of basic GN coverage (E coli, Proteus - thats why sometimes can be used for UTI), also covers Listeria (note: may have certain easy anaerobes coverage but not count as anti-anaerobe since do not cover all the anaerobes)
- Amox-clav now has beta lactamase inhibitor so covers stapha aureus and also has more GN coverage (E coli, Proteus, Klebs, Hemophilus + Moraxella), also covers anaerobes 
- Pip-tazo just adds PsA coverage compared to amox-clav 

Cephalosporins 
- 1st gen = cephalexin (PO), cefadroxil (PO), cefazolin (IV)
	- good strep and staph coverage and some GN (E coli, proteus, klebs), no reliable anaerobic coverage
- 2nd gen = cefotoxitin (IV), cefuroxime (PO/IV), cefprozil (PO)
	- these are not typically used in adult medicine, usually peds since have syrup version
- 3rd gen (BBB starts at this level) = ceftriaxone (IV/IM), cefotaxime (IV - mostly peds as well since can't use CTX cuz biliary excreted and don't want to cause hyperbili in neonates), cefixime (PO - underutilized, oral ceftriaxone, good step down for PNA instead of amox-clav, some people dont believe in it due to penetration issues). 
	- strep, staph and GN (E coli, proteus, hemophilus, moraxella) 
	- can also use this to cover the low risk AmpC (the non-'ECK') 
	- Ceftazidime (IV) is also 3rd gen since crosses BBB. Coverage is good for PsA unlike the other 3rd gens. Pip-tazo (another anti-PsA) does not cover BBB, so thats why use ceftaz instead.  
- 4th gen = cefepime (IV - costly, dont tend to use here) 
	- Covers PsA, ESBL, some AmpC
- 5th gen = ceftobiprole (IV), ceftaroline (IV)
	- Adds MRSA coverage - good if can't use usual MRSA agents
- RULES: none cover enterococcus and none cover anaerobes

Carbapenems
- Ertapenem (IV) = compated to mero no 'APE' - no PsA, may not cover Enterococcus good (only talking about E faecalis, neither cover faecium), no acenetobacter (but rarerly see this)
- Imipenem = not used much, lowers seizure threshold 
- Meropenem (IV) 

Other anti GP 
- Vancomycin (IV) = all GP except VRE obv; + C diff (PO)
- Linezolid (PO/IV) = all GP inc VRE, also has good anti-toxin effect now 
- Clindamycin (PO/IV) = technically covers GP but has been used so much that resistance has emerged, does have anaerobic coverage (oral esp), used now for anti-toxin effect (nec fasc, toxic shock, but now linezolid also has good)

FQs
- Cipro (PO/IV) = mostly GNs, include PsA (only oral abx of PsA), in some cases can also cover ESBL and AmpC if lucky 
- Levofloxacin (PO/IV) = called resp FQ since have more reliable GP coverage in addition to GN, + intracellular organisms (thats why with beta lactams have to add azithro etc but not with FQ) 
- Moxifloxacin (PO/IV) = also a resp FQ similar as levo, has anaerobic coverage (in theory) 

Misc
- Septra =can be used for community acquired MRSA (decent amount of strains from the community are susceptible), sometimes AmpC/ESBL (eg. in UTI if no other options) + weird things like Toxo, pneumocystis (PJP)
- Azithromycin (PO/IV) = mostly used for intracellular organism coverage (usually in PNA) 
- Doxycycline (PO) = also intracellular organisms if can't use azithro, + weird stuff like rickettsia, leptospirosis 
- Nitrofurantoin (PO) = covers ESBL (but only if cystitis) 
- Fosfomycin (PO/IV) = also covers ESBL, IV can be used for resistant GNs 


<u>Septic shock/ undifferentiated sepsis approach </u>
Tazo/ vanco or Mero/ vanco covers lots of these boxes above (G+, G-, anaerobes that are easy and hard) 
Mero covers brain better than tazo 
If dont know whats going on and septic
	1) Rule out the brain - ie. do they have meningitis
		tazo doesnt cover the CNS well so would have to use mero
		also with encephalitis should be covering viruses as well (acyclovir)
	2) Cover the abdomen - if Ø abdo surgery and LEs normal, unlikely 

<u>Syndromes where empiric tx has been shown to give better outcomes</u>
Meningitis 
PNA 
Febrile neutropenia 
Septic shock 
Nec fasc 
SA bacteremia
→ outside of those syndromes, may take some time to think about the treatment; doesn't mean they don't need abx but can just take a bit more time
