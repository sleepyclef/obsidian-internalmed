
<u>Resp/sputum cultres</u>
	Respiratory culture types
		- Sputum = expectorated lower respiratory sample; quality matters. TA = intubated/trach patients, high risk of colonization. BAL = distal airways/alveoli; bronchial wash = more proximal airways.
		- CF cultures use different workup/media because pathogen spectrum differs.
	Common potential pathogens
		- _S. pneumoniae_; _H. influenzae_; _M. catarrhalis_; _S. aureus_; _P. aeruginosa_
		- Hospitalized/ventilated/structural lung disease: _Stenotrophomonas_, _Acinetobacter_, Enterobacterales, etc.
		- Even recognized pathogens (esp. _Pseudomonas_/Enterobacterales) can represent colonization depending on context.
	Interpreting respiratory cultures
		- Respiratory culture ≠ diagnosis of pneumonia → interpret with clinical syndrome + imaging + Gram stain + specimen quality + predominance/quantity of growth.
		- More convincing infection = predominant/heavy growth + corresponding organism on Gram stain + PMNs + good-quality specimen + compatible clinical syndrome.
		- Less convincing = mixed/oropharyngeal flora, poor-quality sputum, organism absent on Gram stain, or chronically intubated/trached patient without compatible clinical change.
	Sputum quality / rejection
		- Squamous epithelial cells (SECs) → saliva/oropharyngeal contamination; PMNs → inflammation.
		- >10 SEC/LPF → generally reject; caveat if WBCs greatly outnumber SECs + see dominant one type of organism
		- Exact criteria are lab-specific; TA specimens are rejected much less consistently.
	Yeast / Candida
		- _Candida_ in sputum/TA/BAL usually = colonization, not Candida pneumonia; even “3+ Candida” does not establish infection.
		- True Candida pneumonia is very uncommon; respiratory culture alone is insufficient even in immunocompromised patients.
		- _Cryptococcus_ is different → true respiratory pathogen; don’t dismiss as colonization.
	Gram stain-culture mismatch
		- Heavy/predominant growth despite negative initial Gram stain (e.g. _Pseudomonas_) → re-review Gram stain; GNRs can be faint/easy to miss among PMNs.
