**Approach to tick bites and prevention:**  
<u>Physical prevention</u>: daily tick check, shower after outdoors, check pets, clothing and gear, walk in center of trails  
<u>Chemical prevention</u>: DEET, picaridin, IR3535, lemon eucalyptus oil, para-menthane-diol, 2-undecanone; 0.5% permethrin for fabrics  
<u>Removal</u>: ASAP, pull **straight out w/o twisting or jerking** w/ clean tweezers, then wash area w/ soap/water or rubbing alcohol  

**Approach to tickborne illness**  
<u>Hx</u>: hiking/outdoor activity, late spring–early autumn. **Most (50–80%) will NOT recall tick bite**  
<u>Sx/signs/labs</u>: fever, myalgias, arthralgias, rash; ↓WBC & PLT, ↑AST & ALT. **Coinfection is common**  

<u>Empiric tx</u>: if high suspicion, send all dx <u>incl. Babesia smear</u> (not tx by doxycycline), **start empiric doxycycline 100 mg PO BID**  
Other zoonoses to consider: tularemia (rabbits), leptospirosis (rats), brucella/coxiella (cows/sheep), though much less common  

**Tick identity and geography (expanding due to climate change)**  
<em>Amblyomma americanum</em> (lone star tick): **Ehrlichia**, STARI, tularemia; widely found in eastern US, more common in the south  
<em>Dermacentor variabilis</em> (American dog tick): **RMSF**, tularemia; East of Rocky Mtns. (particularly southeast), northern Mexico  
<em>Ixodes scapularis</em> (black-legged tick): **Lyme, Anaplasma, Ehrlichia, Babesia, Powassan, relapsing fever.** NE, Mid-Atlantic, central Midwest
