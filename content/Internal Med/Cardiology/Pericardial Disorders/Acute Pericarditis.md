<font color="#938953">ACUTE PERICARDITIS</font>
sources = EMCrit IBCC (2024), Emergency Medicine Cases

approach to suspected pericarditis  
	diagnosis of exclusion → first rule out ACS, PE, dissection, esophageal rupture, tension PTX  
	EM Cases pearl = avoid premature closure; MI can cause diffuse STE, and pericarditis can have normal ECG  
	assess for tamponade, myocardial involvement, high-risk features, nonidiopathic etiology

diagnosis  
	requires ≥2/4 criteria (performance of criteria not well established)  
	1) chest pain (~90%)  
		sharp, pleuritic, positional (better sitting forward, worse supine), +/- worse swallowing  
		usually central, may be localized; radiation to trapezius ridge (classic), shoulder, L arm  
		may be severe, mimic MI; onset often sudden, lasting hours-days  
		pain often absent with subacute etiologies (TB, malignancy, uremia, radiation)  
		associated viral prodrome, low-grade fever (~15%), dyspnea (~33%, often splinting) 
	2) pericardial rub (~30%)  
		highly specific, poorly sensitive; best at LLSB leaning forward  
		1-3 components (ventricular systole, early diastole, atrial contraction)  
	3) ECG changes  
		diffuse STE and/or PR depression (including isolated PR depression)  
		classic stage 1 changes ~60%; any ECG abnormality perhaps ~80% (IBCC)  
	4) new/worsening pericardial effusion (~60%)  
		absence does not exclude pericarditis; presence alone not diagnostic  
		supportive = ↑ CRP/ESR/WBC, pericardial inflammation on CT/CMR  
		criteria do NOT distinguish pericarditis from perimyocarditis

ECG  
	4 stages (timing variable, may skip stages → normal ECG despite acute pericarditis)  
		1 = diffuse concave STE + PR depression  
		2 = normalization of ST/PR  
		3 = diffuse TWI after ST normalization  
		4 = T-wave normalization  
	pericarditis vs STEMI  
		diffuse concave STE, PR depression, preserved upright T waves in stage 1  
		STE rarely >5 mm; STD in aVR/V1 favours pericarditis  
		STE II > III favours pericarditis; III > II suggests inferior STEMI  
		STD in aVL strongly suggests inferior STEMI (pericarditis usually STE in aVL)  
		sharper J-point in pericarditis vs blurred in MI (EM Cases)  
		Spodick sign = downsloping TP segment/terminal PR depression (best II, lateral leads)  
			supportive but not specific; also seen in ~5% STEMI (EM Cases)  
		no single ECG feature reliably excludes MI  
	pericarditis vs early repolarization  
		STE/T-wave amplitude ratio >0.25 in V6 favours pericarditis  
		early repol = J-point notching, no PR depression, usually stable ECG  
	large effusion = low QRS voltage, +/- electrical alternans  
	uremic pericarditis = often normal ECG (less epicardial inflammation)

initial investigations  
	ECG, serial trop if ACS/myocarditis concern, CBC/diff, CRP/ESR, Cr, TTE, CXR  
	trop = may be modestly ↑ with myopericarditis; usually lower than MI/myocarditis  
		EM Cases pearl = trop often relatively stable over hours in pericarditis vs rising in acute MI (not definitive)  
	CRP >3 mg/L (~80% sensitivity); may be normal if 12h (IBCC)  
		very high CRP → ? significant myocardial involvement (EM Cases)  
		usually normalizes within 1-2 weeks of tx  
		persistently ↑ CRP → ↑ recurrence/constriction risk; trend to guide tx/taper  
	WBC mild ↑ common; >13-14 → ? nonidiopathic etiology (infection, malignancy, etc.)  
	Cr → assess uremia, guide NSAID/colchicine safety  
	TTE → effusion, tamponade, ventricular fxn (↓ LVEF → ? perimyocarditis)  
	CXR → PNA/TB, enlarged silhouette if large effusion  
	CMR if uncertain dx, suspected clinically significant myocarditis, poor echo windows  
		pericardial LGE (~95% sensitivity, not fully specific) = inflammation/neovascularization  
		T2 edema + LGE = acute inflammation  
		LGE without edema = subacute/chronic inflammation  
		resolution of both = resolved inflammation

pericardial effusion  
	POCUS size = maximal echo-free space at end-diastole  
		trivial = only visible in systole  
		small = <10 mm; moderate = 10–20 mm  
		large = 21–25 mm; very large = >25 mm (EM Cases)  
	>20 mm = ↑ tamponade risk, suggests nonidiopathic etiology (malignancy, TB, chronic inflammation)  
	complex/heterogeneous effusion → ? malignancy/infection  
	effusion size alone does NOT determine tamponade  
	rapid accumulation of small effusion → tamponade; slow accumulation of large effusion may be tolerated

cardiac tamponade  
	clinical diagnosis, not purely an imaging diagnosis  
	↑ intrapericardial pressure → impaired R heart filling → ↓ preload/CO  
	EM Cases pearl = do NOT exclude tamponade due to small/moderate effusion or wait for hypotension before drainage  
	clinical findings  
		dyspnea (~87-89%), tachycardia (~77%), ↑ JVP (~76%), cardiomegaly on CXR (~89%)  
		pulsus paradoxus = inspiratory ↓ SBP >10 mmHg (sensitivity ~82%)  
		EM Cases pearl = rapid assessment by palpating radial pulse for inspiratory weakening (qualitative)  
		absence of pulsus paradoxus argues against tamponade in typical circumstances, not absolute  
	POCUS findings  
		RA systolic collapse = earliest sign  
		RV diastolic collapse = high specificity  
		dilated/plethoric IVC with minimal respiratory variation = high sensitivity  
		exaggerated respiratory variation in MV/TV Doppler inflow  
		large effusion >25 mm supportive, not required  
	if diagnosis uncertain  
		stable → STAT formal echo, +/- arterial line (assess respiratory BP variation)  
		EM Cases = cautious fluid challenge may improve preload/unmask findings; avoid with severe LV dysfunction  
		pericardiocentesis can be diagnostic + therapeutic  
	tamponade or concerning evolving physiology → urgent cardiology involvement/pericardial drainage  
	EM Cases pitfall = delaying pericardiocentesis until overt hemodynamic collapse

risk stratification / disposition  
	major predictors of complicated pericarditis (IBCC)  ie. will need admission & w/u for specific etiology
		fever >38°C, subacute onset, effusion >20 mm, tamponade, no NSAID response after ≥1 week  
	minor predictors  
		myocarditis, immunosuppression, trauma, oral AC  
	high-risk features, uncertain/nonviral etiology, significant myocardial involvement → admit/extended w/u  
	uncomplicated presumed viral/idiopathic disease, mild sx, no high-risk features → usually outpatient  
	NSAID + colchicine, exercise restriction, follow-up within 1 week (EM Cases)  
	counsel = continue meds despite early symptomatic improvement → prevent recurrence

etiology / targeted w/u  
	idiopathic/viral = most common (often unconfirmed viral etiology)  
	infectious = viral (enterovirus, influenza, COVID, HIV etc.), TB, Lyme, bacterial/purulent, fungal (rare)  
	autoimmune = SLE, RA, systemic sclerosis, Sjogren, vasculitis, IBD, Still's, sarcoid  
	metabolic = uremia, severe hypothyroidism  
	malignancy = lung, breast, hematologic  
	post-cardiac injury = MI, cardiac surgery/procedures, trauma  
	medications = hydralazine, procainamide, isoniazid (drug-induced lupus); chemo/ICI, mesalamine, clozapine, etc.  
	radiation  
	features suggesting nonidiopathic disease  
		subacute/chronic onset, fever >38, immunosuppression, large effusion, myocarditis, recurrence or NSAID failure  
		focused hx = CTD sx (arthritis, rash, Raynaud, sicca, weakness), TB risk/exposure, drugs, malignancy  
		+/- TSH, eosinophils, ANA/RF, HIV/HCV, Lyme, TB w/u based on suspicion  
		ANA only if other CTD features (low-titre ANA common in idiopathic pericarditis)

idiopathic/viral pericarditis — treatment  
	1st line = high-dose ASA/NSAID + colchicine  
		NSAID = control acute inflammation; colchicine = prevent recurrence  
			EM Cases pearl = NSAIDs relieve sx but have not demonstrated prevention of recurrence; colchicine ↓ recurrence by up to ~50%  
			NSAID/ASA must be anti-inflammatory doses (underdosing → analgesia without treating inflammation → recurrence)  
			ibuprofen 600-800 mg PO TID (IBCC; EM Cases 600 mg TID)  
			indomethacin 25-50 mg PO TID (IBCC; EM Cases 50 mg TID)  
			ASA 750-1000 mg PO TID (IBCC)  
			no clear efficacy difference → choose safest agent  
			ASA preferred post-MI (NSAIDs impair infarct healing), coronary stents  
			IBCC also favours ASA in older age/AKI risk; still assess renal + GI toxicity  
		PPI = IBCC recommends BID for high-dose anti-inflammatory therapy  
			EM Cases particularly emphasizes PPI in high UGIB risk/indomethacin use  
	full-dose NSAID/ASA ~7-14 days → taper over ~3-4 weeks guided by sx/CRP (IBCC)  
	EM Cases = uncomplicated disease often 1-2 weeks before taper  
	IBCC cites possible benefit from continuing tx ~1 month after pain resolution to ↓ constriction risk (limited evidence)  
	sx usually improve within days; no response after ~1 week → reassess dx/etiology  
	additional analgesia = acetaminophen +/- opioids if severe pain  
	restrict strenuous exercise until recovery (EM Cases)

colchicine  
	0.6 mg PO daily if 4-6 weeks but 3 months; recurrent = new episode after >1 month symptom-free  
	myopericarditis = ↑ trop with preserved cardiac fxn  
	perimyocarditis = myocardial dysfunction → more concerning; manage similarly to myocarditis  
	~15% acute pericarditis have concomitant myocarditis (IBCC)  
	~1/3 recur within 1.5 years (IBCC)  
	complications = recurrent pericarditis, AF, tamponade, constrictive pericarditis
