
<u>Revascularization</u> 
step 1 - indication for revasc? 
	a) survival/prognostic benefit
		- LM ≥50% (old VA/CASS/ECSS)
		- MVD + EF ≤35% (STICH/STICHES, only CABG)
		(+/- MVD w EF >35% to ↓ MI/events but Ø mortality benefit, esp if 3VD or 2VD w pLAD, SYNTAX>22)
	b) refractory symptoms
	c) ACS - to save myocardium from culprit, also to ↓ recurrent events from non-culprits
		- STEMI → immediate culprit PCI; if MVD - need complete revasc (COMPLETE); PCI now or staged (COMPLETE), or CABG if preferred 
		- NSTE-ACS → culprit PCI; if MVD - can PCI now or staged, or if stable can defer all even culprit PCI for CABG if preferred
step 2 - PCI vs CABG? 
	1-2VD, simple = PCI 
	simple LM = PCI possible (EXCEL, NOBLE)
	complex LM = CABG
	MVD w low complexity, no DM+LAD = PCI
	MVD w high complexity (SYNTAX >33) = CABG
	MVD + EF ≤35% = CABG (as above for prognostic benefit)
	MVD w LAD + DM = CABG regardless of SYNTAX/complexity (FREEDOM)

When to consider revascularization? 
	a) to improve <u>survival</u>
		1. <mark style="background:rgba(3, 135, 102, 0.2)">left main dz ≥50% </mark>
			ACC: <font color="#76923c">class 1</font> for CABG, class 2a for PCI if can provide equiv revasc
			metanalysis of older CASS, ECSS, VA trials showed ~75% mortality reduction w CABG 
			CABG vs PCI? can be either depending on anatomy 
				![[Chronic Coronary Syndrome, Stable CAD-1791057446272.webp|289]]
		2. <mark style="background:rgba(3, 135, 102, 0.2)">MVD (ie. 2-3VD ≥70%), EF 35-% (severe LV dysfunction; ie. ischemic CMP)</mark>
			ACC <font color="#76923c">class 1</font> w CABG; ESC class 2a w CABG
			STICH/STICHES trial 
				EF <35%, CABG vs OMT
				59% vs 66% mortality at 10y = 7% mortalitity reduction at 10y (barely 1% per yr) 
				but actually no sig benefit at 5y
				ie. late and marginal benefit w CABG; that is why ESC give it class 2a 
				3VD EF<27% derive the most benefit
			CABG vs PCI? 
				As of current evidence, mortality benefit is only with CABG (STICH), possible PCI ? (pending STICH3C)
				Newer trial REVIVED-BCIS2 (NEJM Aug 2022)
					EF<35%, CAD supplying >50% myocardium (via Jeopardy score) + viability, PCI v OMT
					mainly 2 and 3VD, even LM dz was included (14%)
					PCI not superior to OMT in terms of mortality, HF hosp, MI; hypothesis is that once advanced myocardial dz/HF occurs d/t CAD, bad outcomes mostly result from poor mycardial conditions and less dependent on CAD 
		 3. <mark style="background:rgba(240, 200, 0, 0.2)">MVD (ie. 2-3VD ≥70%), normal EF</mark>
			ACC: <font color="#c4bd97">class 2b</font> for CABG or ?PCI if suitable
			metanalysis of CASS, ECSS, VA study showed ~40% mortality reduction w CABG, irrespective of LV function, but moreso if mild LV dysfunction 35-50%
			revascularization may reduce spontaneous MI / urgent revascularization, despite uncertain survival benefit.
			modern trials (BARI2D, ISCHEMIA) question the CABG mortality benefit; maybe provides some M reduction benefit if high atherosclerotic burden 
				![[Chronic Coronary Syndrome, Stable CAD-1791055862176.webp|324]]
			CABG vs PCI?
				![[Chronic Coronary Syndrome, Stable CAD-1791067944167.webp|312]]
				FREEDOM is the trial for diabetes
				![[Chronic Coronary Syndrome, Stable CAD-1791068812719.webp|265]]
	b) to improve <u>symptoms</u>; if severe refractory angina
		ACC: class 1 for both CABG or PCI if suitable
		refrctory angina = freq angina (multiple times weekly) that persists despite 2 anti-anginals (or less if intolerant d/t low BP/HR)
		CABG vs PCI (basically summary of recommendations as above)
			1-2VD → PCI (unless v complex)
			LM dz → CABG generally preferred; PCI reasonable with suitable low/intermediate-complexity anatomy
			MVD w high complexity (SYNTAX >33) → favor CABG
			MVD w LAD + DM → favor CABG (FREEDOM)
			high surgical risk / unsuitable for CABG → PCI reasonable

c) In the context of ACS
	ACS itself provides the indication to revascularize the culprit lesion
		STEMI: immediate culprit PCI 
		NSTE-ACS: invasive strategy based on risk; once significant culprit anatomy is identified, revascularize with PCI or CABG depending on urgency/ anatomy
	ACS + multivessel disease
		In stable patients, generally aim for complete revascularization of significant disease
		For nonculprit lesions after ACS, the evidence supports complete revascularization mainly to reduce recurrent MI and future ischemic events, not because an isolated mortality benefit has been definitively proven.
			In COMPLETE (STEMI + MVD), complete revascularization reduced the composite of CV death or MI from 10.5% to 7.8%, but that was driven by fewer MIs. CV death alone was essentially unchanged: 2.9% vs 3.2% (HR 0.93).
		STEMI + MVD
			COMPLETE: complete revascularization ↓ CV death/ MI vs culprit-only PCI
			simple residual anatomy → PCI, same sitting or staged
			complex residual LM/3VD → culprit PCI first, then consider CABG for remaining disease
	    NSTE-ACS + MVD
		    if straightforward anatomy → multivessel PCI reasonable
		    if stable + surgical anatomy → may stop after diagnostic cath and proceed to CABG rather than stenting first
    Factors favoring CABG in ACS
	    complex LM
	    diffuse/complex MVD
	    diabetes + MVD involving LAD
	    severe LV dysfunction + complex MVD
	    inability to achieve durable/complete PCI
    Cardiogenic shock
	    ACS + shock + MVD → culprit-only PCI initially
	    CULPRIT-SHOCK: immediate multivessel PCI caused more death/RRT than culprit-only PCI
	    reassess nonculprit disease after stabilization
