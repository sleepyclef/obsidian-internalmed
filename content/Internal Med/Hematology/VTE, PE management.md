<font color="#938953"><u>Pulmonary embolism</u></font>
source: ibcc 

risk factors
	strong = ortho (hip/ leg #, hip/knee replacement); srg/trauma; cardiac (ADHF/MI 3mo); prior VTE 
	mod = arthroscopic knee, paralytic stroke, CVC, malig (esp mets, +/- chemo), CHF/resp issue, postpartum, >80yo, thrombophilia
	weak = bed rest >3d, prolonged air/ car, lap srg, obesity, antepartum, varicose veins, DM/HTN

risk factors  
	strong = ortho (hip/leg #, hip/knee replacement); major srg/trauma, SCI; cardiac (CHF/AF admit or MI within 3mo); prior VTE  
	mod = arthroscopic knee, paralytic stroke, CVC, malig (esp mets, +/- chemo), CHF/resp failure, postpartum, OCP/HRT, IVF, >80yo, thrombophilia, autoimmune dz/IBD, infxn  
	weak = bed rest >3d, prolonged air/car travel, lap srg, obesity, antepartum, varicose veins, DM/HTN, older age

clinical presentation  
	massive/submassive (RV strain/failure) = syncope/presyncope, bradycardia, PEA arrest, anginal CP, diaphoresis; POCUS RV dilation, CXR usually clear  
	large central PE = acute dyspnea, hypoxemia; CXR often clear, +/- effusion  
	pulm infarction = smaller distal PE, pleuritic CP, hemoptysis (usually mild), low-grade fever, +/- dyspnea  
	pulm infarct imaging = pleural-based wedge opacity (usually LL), +/- GGO/reverse halo, small ipsilateral effusion (~60%), resolves over 3–5wk  
	DVT = 50% asymptomatic; calf pain, unilateral swelling, tenderness, pitting edema, erythema/warmth

D-dimer  
	high sensitivity (~98%), poor specificity (↑ infxn, inflammation, malig, srg/trauma, preg, age, cirrhosis)  
	cutoff = FEU <500 mcg/L (0.5 mg/L); DDU <250 mcg/L  
	age-adjusted (>50yo) = age × 10 FEU or ~age × 5 DDU (mcg/L; assay dependent)  
	neg D-dimer excludes PE in low/mod PTP; high PTP → imaging  
	false neg possible if delayed presentation (>2wk); low utility in hospitalized/critically ill

investigations  
	ECG, CXR, POCUS → alternative causes of dyspnea/shock  
	ABG = not diagnostic; normal ABG/O2 does not exclude PE  
	CXR = often normal; +/- wedge opacity, small effusion if pulm infarct  
	LE Doppler = sensitivity ~40% in PE; +DVT confirms VTE (may obviate CTPA if tx same); neg does NOT exclude PE  
	CTPA = first-line, >95% sensitivity central/segmental PE; less reliable SSPE; may identify alternate dx  
	CTPA acute clot = intraluminal filling defect, contrast surrounding clot, acute angle with vessel wall  
	indeterminate CTPA = review with radiology, assess contrast/timing, +/- LE Doppler, rpt CTPA if needed  
	filling defect DDx = acute PE, CTEPH, in situ thrombosis (severe PH), tumor emboli/PA sarcoma, foreign body, artifact  
	V/Q = alternative if CTPA unsuitable; best if normal CXR; normal excludes PE, high-prob supports PE, intermediate nondiagnostic

diagnostic approach  
	assess pretest prob → Wells/clinical gestalt; PERC only if very low risk in ED (not inpatient)  
	Wells PE = DVT signs 3, PE most likely 3, HR >100 1.5, immobilization/srg <4wk 1.5, prior VTE 1.5, hemoptysis 1, active malig 1  
	Wells 0–1 low, 2–6 mod, >6 high  
	PERC = age <50, HR <100, SpO2 ≥95%, no hemoptysis, estrogen, recent srg/trauma, prior VTE, unilateral leg swelling; all neg → no testing if very low PTP  
	YEARS = DVT signs, hemoptysis, PE most likely; 0 criteria → D-dimer <1000 FEU excludes; ≥1 → <500 FEU excludes  
	low/mod PTP → D-dimer → CTPA if positive  
	high PTP → direct CTPA  
	inpatient suspected DVT = LE Doppler directly if likely DVT or expected +D-dimer

management (DVT / low-risk PE)  
	empiric AC not routine if CTPA imminent; start if high PTP or significant imaging delay and low bleed risk  
	LMWH preferred over UFH if stable; avoid/caution if CrCl <30, high bleed risk, planned procedure  
	UFH = if severe renal impairment, rapid interruption/reversal needed  
	DOAC = preferred for eligible stable PE, esp outpatient (apixaban/rivaroxaban)  
	low-risk PE = sPESI 0, HESTIA 0, clinically stable → possible outpatient tx  
	AC duration = at least 3mo; extended if unprovoked/persistent RF (weigh bleed risk)  
	RV dysfunction, ↑trop/BNP, ↑lactate, hypotension → higher-risk PE; monitoring/PERT, assess advanced therapies

special situations  
	isolated SSPE = confirm with radiology (false + common), review PTP, LE +/- UE Doppler, +/- D-dimer; no DVT + low recurrence risk → surveillance possible rather than AC; rpt LE Doppler 5–7d  
	distal calf DVT = AC if low bleed risk/high extension risk; otherwise serial Doppler, AC if proximal propagation  
	PE + mild hemoptysis = usually capillary bleeding from pulm infarct, not contraindication to AC; severe hemoptysis → reassess source/bleed risk  
	suspected PE + shock = urgent bedside echo/POCUS, assess RV failure, rapid definitive imaging if feasible, immediate senior/PERT involvement

<font color="#938953">SICKER PE</font>

<u>step 1</u> - investigations  
	INR/PTT, fibrinogen, D-dimer, trop, lactate, ECG, CTPA  
	CTPA ASAP if stable enough; difficult to have meaningful PERT discussion without CT

<u>step 2</u> - risk stratification  
	IBCC proposed criteria for intermediate/high-risk (ALL required):  
		(i) acute RV dilation/strain (RV/LV >1, ideally new)  
		(ii)↑ trop (or ischemic ECG changes if trop unavailable); neg trop + normal ECG argues strongly against severe PE (but early trop may be neg)  
		(iii) substantial clot burden capable of causing RV dysfx  (note: if burden small w RV dysfxn, suggests incidental PE in context of chronic RV dysfxn)
		(iv) PE primary cause of instability (not alternative process)  
	high-risk if ANY:  
		SBP <90 or drop >40 for >15min, pressors, persistent bradycardia, cardiac arrest  
		lactate >4 may qualify (controversial; exclude other causes)  
	intermediate-actionable (vs observable) = multiple concerning features, likely benefit from reperfusion:  
		lactate >>2 (esp >4), shocked appearance/poor perfusion, shock index >>1  
		borderline BP (SBP <110, MAP <80), transient hypotension  
		syncope/presyncope (alone insufficient)  
		CT grade 2-3 IVC contrast reflux, RV/LV >1.5, large clot-in-transit  
	intermediate-observable = no significant features of impending collapse → AC alone

classification pearls  
	PE risk continuous/dynamic, rigid categories artificial  
	ESC 2019 intermediate-high = includes stable pts + normotensive shock nearing collapse → doesn't guide management  
	IBCC prefers intermediate-observable vs intermediate-actionable; no validated cutoff, clinical judgement  
	2026 AHA/ACC A-E2 (10 subgroups) = IBCC does NOT recommend clinical use (complex, insufficient evidence)
		![[VTE, PE management-1795035591446.webp|315]]

clinical assessment  
	syncope/presyncope = ↓ hemodynamic reserve; alone not necessarily high risk  
	sx >10-14d = organized clot, less responsive to lysis; stable sx for days reassuring vs recent/accelerating sx  
	diaphoresis/clammy = endogenous epi maintaining perfusion → ominous  
	hypoperfusion = cap refill >3s, mottling, confusion/agitation (AMS very late)  
	bradycardia esp <40 = ominous, impending brady-asystolic arrest  
	shock index (HR/SBP) >1 = ↓ reserve  
	transient SBP <90 (<15min) = strong predictor deterioration (OR ~6)  
	HTN does NOT exclude severe PE (endogenous epi surge may maintain/↑ BP despite poor perfusion)

biomarkers/ECG  
	lactate >2 = ↑ mortality/collapse (~10% absolute risk), >4 particularly concerning  
	trop ↑ = RV injury, ↑ mortality (~5x); hs-trop low-level elevations may be overly sensitive/nonspecific  
	ECG = Qr V1, STE aVR/V1/III, RBBB, S1Q3T3, RAD, STD V4-V6, TWI precordial/inferior  
	normal ECG argues against severe PE  
	BNP = nonspecific (CHF/CKD); limited value for acute RV risk

CT/echo  
	clot burden alone ≠ severity; small PE + RV failure → consider alternative cause/chronic PH  
	CT = RV/LV >1, septal bowing, IVC dilation/contrast reflux  
	RV/LV >1.5 concerning; grade 2-3 IVC reflux possibly stronger predictor  
	echo = RV>LV, ↓ TAPSE <18mm, D-sign, +/- clot-in-transit  
	RV wall >5mm → chronic PH; nondilated IVC → instability unlikely solely PE  
	IBCC prefers CT for PERT (accessible, operator-independent); POCUS adds little prognostic value after CT, may delay decisions; useful for alternative shock causes  
	avoid double-counting same RV strain on CT + echo

BOVA  score
	SBP 90-100 (2), ↑ trop (2), RV dysfx (2), HR >110 (1)  
	0-2 = ~3% PE mortality, conservative tx; 3-4 = ~7%, grey zone (IBCC suggests ignore); 5-7 = ~10%, supports aggressive tx  
	best physiology-based score per IBCC but weak discrimination (AUC ~0.64), poor sensitivity, trop threshold not standardized  
	PESI/sPESI = 30d all-cause mortality, NOT imminent PE collapse; overweights age/comorbidities  
	IBCC favours clinical judgement + raw physiologic data over scores

<u>step 3</u> - thrombolytic contraindications  
	absolute = active serious bleed, hx hemorrhagic CVA, ischemic CVA <3mo, intracranial AVM, recent CNS (brain/spinal) srg, major head trauma with brain injury <3wk  
	relative = CNS tumor (depends on type), major non-CNS srg <2-3wk, internal bleed <4wk, noncompressible vascular puncture  
	relative = Plt <100, INR >1.7 on warfarin, fibrinogen <1.5g/L, OAC/multiple antithrombotics  
	relative = BP >180/110, severe chronic HTN, age >65, dementia, pregnancy/1st wk postpartum, IE, advanced cirrhosis, low body weight  
	review prior brain imaging, coags, hemorrhage hx  
	absolute vs relative contextual; impending death from PE may justify lysis despite usual CI if no alternatives

PE-CH score (ICH risk with lysis)  
	PVD 1, age >65 1, prior MI 1, CVA with residual deficit 5  
	0 = 1.2% ICH; 1 = 2.9%; 2 = 3.4%; ≥5 = 18%  
	retrospective, may overestimate tPA-related ICH; misses rare major RF (eg recent neurosrg)

<u>step 4</u> - treatment approach  
	intermediate-observable = AC alone, +/- high-level telemetry, repeat trop/lactate/ECG  
	intermediate-actionable = PERT, +/- IR mechanical thrombectomy or reduced-dose tPA (18-50mg)  
	high-risk, no lytic CI = tPA 100mg over 2h (+/- 20mg bolus if crashing); 50mg option esp elderly  
	high-risk, relative lytic CI = reduced-dose tPA 25-50mg over 2h (may rpt based on response/coags) OR IR thrombectomy  
	high-risk, absolute lytic CI = IR thrombectomy, surgical thrombectomy, VA-ECMO  
	actively dying + no alternatives → +/- 25-50mg tPA despite absolute CI (IBCC opinion)

RESUS

pathophys  
	PE → ↑ PVR/RV afterload → RV failure → ↓ LV filling/CO → hypotension → ↓ RCA perfusion → worsening RV failure  
	terminal event often brady-asystolic arrest

oxygen  
	100% FiO2 = pulm vasodilator; NRB 15L/min + NC 15L/min OR HFNC  
	avoid intubation if possible (PPV ↑ RV afterload/↓ preload → arrest); if unavoidable, minimize airway pressures/PEEP

vasopressors (IBCC preference)  
	epi = 1st line inopressor, start early, titrate MAP >>65 (no fixed max)  
	β agonism → ↑ RV contractility/CO, chronotropy (prevent brady-asystolic arrest), possible pulm vasodilation; ↑ MAP → ↑ RCA perfusion  
	vasopressin = 2nd line if refractory, 0.04-0.08 U/min  
	peri-arrest = 1U IV bolus, rpt q2-5min PRN (IBCC nonstandard rescue)  
	systemic vasoconstriction + pulm vasodilation; weak/slow titration (t½ ~20min)

inhaled pulmonary vasodilators (IBCC rescue)  
	use whichever available fastest; iNO + epoprostenol may combine if refractory (different mechanisms)  
	iNO = 20ppm via HFNC/vent OR 20-50ppm nasal prongs  
	epoprostenol = 50ng/kg/min inhaled initially (max), wean  
	nebulized NTG = 5mg over 15min, rpt PRN (lasts ~30min)  
	nebulized milrinone = 5mg, rpt q3-4h; peri-arrest 2.5mg via ETT  
	NTG/milrinone = off-label, limited evidence; bridge while awaiting iNO/epo  
	iNO vs epo = no clear superiority

preload  
	fluids rarely helpful (already ↑ RV filling pressures); excess → RV dilation, septal shift, ↓ CO  
	only give fluid if clear low filling pressures (eg small IVC, unusual in severe PE → reconsider cause of shock)  
	substantial prior fluids + systemic congestion → +/- diuresis

TX DETAILS

anticoagulation  
	heparin prevents new clot formation, does NOT lyse existing clot  
	IBCC notes limited high-quality RCT evidence for heparin in PE; early-heparin mortality studies likely biased  
	LMWH generally preferred low/intermediate-low PE (↓ bleeding)  
	UFH may be preferable intermediate-high/high-risk = rapid interruption/reversal if bleeding, titratable for lysis/procedures, LMWH absorption unreliable in shock  
	heparin given with/immediately before tPA = ↑ bleeding without proven added benefit (IBCC opinion)

thrombolysis  
	accepted for high-risk PE; controversial intermediate-risk  
	goal = ↓ PVR/RV strain to prevent collapse, NOT complete clot clearance or prevention of CTEPH  
	PEITHO = ↓ hemodynamic collapse but ↑ bleeding/ICH; no long-term CTEPH benefit  
	IBCC argues standard 100mg tPA likely excessive:  
		all infused tPA passes through pulm arteries (vs small fraction reaching coronaries in MI)  
		t½ ~4min → repeatedly circulates in pulm system  
		PE obstruction entirely clot (vs MI plaque + clot)  
		partial dissolution sufficient to prevent RV failure  
	fixed doses flawed (variable fibrinolytic response); slow/titrated lysis may be preferable if not actively crashing (IBCC opinion)
	-
	full-dose alteplase = 100mg IV over 2h; crashing → 10-20mg IV bolus then remainder over 2h  
	half-dose = 0.5mg/kg IV (max 50mg), 10mg bolus → remainder over 2h (MOPETT/IBCC)  
	-
	IBCC favours lower doses in selected pts, possibly similar efficacy with less bleeding; not definitively proven  
	slow peripheral alteplase = 18mg over 6h (3mg/h) (IBCC proposed, nonstandard)  
	rationale = gradual ↓ PVR with potentially less bleeding  
	low-dose trials suggest ↓ cardiopulm deterioration; ICH still possible, hard clinical outcome benefit uncertain  
	STRATIFY = peripheral 20mg tPA similar to catheter-directed 20mg → IBCC argues peripheral route avoids unnecessary catheter risks  
	alternative slow regimen = 2mg/h ×6-12h (12-24mg total); monitor CBC/fibrinogen/PTT/anti-Xa q6h; stop/reassess if bleeding or fibrinogen <1.5g/L  
	TNK = <60kg 30mg, 60-70 35mg, 70-80 40mg, 80-90 45mg, >90 50mg IV bolus (off-label for PE)

heparin + tPA  
	optimal timing uncertain; ideally stop heparin/allow clearance before full/half-dose lysis if feasible, but don't delay life-saving tPA  
	tPA short t½ BUT persistent hypofibrinogenemia, fibrinogen degradation coagulopathy, platelet dysfx, ongoing plasmin activity  
	traditional UFH restart = PTT <1.5-2x normal, no bolus; IBCC suggests delaying longer if good response/bleeding risk  
	IBCC slow 18mg/6h regimen = LMWH 1mg/kg SC q12h may continue OR UFH reduced to 300-600 U/h during infusion (trial-derived, nonstandard)

mechanical thrombectomy  
	IBCC opinion = true benefit of IR is physical clot removal rather than catheter-directed tPA  
	FlowTriever = strongest evidence; improved RV strain/hemodynamics, low adverse events  
	PEERLESS = superior to catheter-directed tPA on composite outcomes, fewer ICU admits/shorter LOS; NOT compared with AC alone  
	Penumbra Indigo = less evidence, RV improvement but limited clinical outcome data  
	AngioVac = best for IVC/RV clot, PA access difficult/riskier  
	AngioJet = avoid (FDA black box warning: bradycardia, massive hemoptysis, renal failure)

surgical thrombectomy  
	historically considered excessively dangerous + ineffective → fell out of favour for years  
	resurgence over past decade with improved outcomes; mortality ~10%, reasonable in selected pts at very high PE mortality risk  
	key indication = clot straddling PFO → fragmentation could cause immediate systemic embolism/stroke; surgery may be front-line

VA-ECMO  
	high-risk PE potentially excellent candidates (reversible RV failure if supported)  
	BUT major complications: stroke ~10%, limb ischemia ~8%; neuro risk may exceed ICH risk from systemic tPA  
	roles = c/i to lytic, stabilization before intubation, refractory shock despite lysis  
	can also be used as bridge to endogenous clot dissolution on AC, gradual tPA (1-2mg/h), or thrombectomy/surgery  
	limited availability; no high-quality RCT evidence

clot-in-transit  
	mimics = Eustachian valve, RV trabeculations/moderator band; true large clot = thick, mobile, snake-like  
	small clot = often incidental, uncertain significance, may not alter tx  
	large mobile clot = likely further embolization → systemic lysis often effective, IR extraction if lytic CI  
	PFO-straddling clot = paradoxical embolism/stroke risk; lysis relative CI (fragmentation), surgery often preferred

refractory hypoxemia  
	hypoxemia despite ~100% FiO2 = shunt physiology → R→L PFO/ASD (↑ RA pressure) OR concurrent lung pathology (PNA, mucus plug, PTX)  
	bubble echo +/- CXR/CT  
	HFNC 100%, inhaled pulm vasodilators, definitive PE tx  
	intubation won't fix intracardiac shunt, may worsen (↑ PVR/right-sided pressures → ↑ shunting)

PE cardiac arrest  
	usually PEA/brady-asystolic; IBCC reports survival >50% possible with aggressive resuscitation  
	alteplase = 50mg IV bolus (IBCC preferred code dose); 100mg also reasonable per IBCC  
	TNK weight-based IV bolus alternative  
	IBCC favours lysis despite usual CI unless immediate ECMO available  
	post-ROSC epi infusion early (eg 20mcg/min, titrate) → avoid re-arrest once epi bolus wears off  
	minimize airway pressures, +/- inhaled pulm vasodilators via ETT  
	prolonged CPR 60-90min after lysis to allow circulation/effect

IVC filters  
	may ↓ recurrent PE but ↑ DVT/filter thrombosis, no mortality benefit (PREPIC trials)  
	risks = filter thrombosis, IVC wall penetration (~19%, usually tolerated), migration/fracture  
	IBCC = NO filter if AC possible, even high-risk PE  
	if AC contraindicated → evidence lacking; may consider only if ALL:  
	unable to AC + intermediate/high-risk PE with poor reserve + large proximal/free-floating DVT  
	retrievable filter, remove ASAP (most aren't actually retrieved)
